# Incident Classification Framework

## Purpose

The incident classification framework provides a consistent method for
categorizing and prioritizing security incidents.

---

## Incident Categories

### Phishing

Credential theft, malicious links, impersonation or social engineering.

### Malware

Malicious software including trojans, ransomware, spyware or worms.

### Account Compromise

Unauthorized access to user, administrator or service accounts.

### Data Breach

Unauthorized disclosure, access, modification or loss of sensitive data.

### Insider Threat

Malicious or negligent activity by an employee, contractor or trusted user.

### Vulnerability Exploitation

Unauthorized activity resulting from exploitation of a technical vulnerability.

### Availability Incident

Security-related disruption affecting system or service availability.

### Third-Party Incident

Security incident originating from or materially affecting a supplier or service provider.

---

# Severity Levels

| Severity | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Limited impact and no significant data/security exposure | Single blocked phishing email |
| Medium | Moderate impact requiring coordinated response | Compromised standard user account |
| High | Significant business/security impact | Confirmed malware affecting multiple systems |
| Critical | Severe business, regulatory or security impact | Ransomware or major data breach |

---

# Severity Criteria

Severity should consider:

- Business impact
- Confidentiality impact
- Integrity impact
- Availability impact
- Data exposure
- Regulatory impact
- Number of affected users/assets
- Privilege level
- Potential financial impact
- Reputational impact
